# 🌀 TORI — Game Concept Document

## Logline
A mystical 2D/3D exploration game where players discover and align ancient torii gates to open portals between hidden realms, unraveling the secrets of a forgotten spiritual network.

---

## 🎮 Core Loop
1. **Explore** — Wander atmospheric environments (forests, mountains, shrines, ruins)
2. **Discover** — Find torii gates, some visible, some hidden or broken
3. **Attune** — Solve environmental puzzles to align a gate (orientation, offerings, timing)
4. **Jump** — Step through an activated gate to teleport to a new, connected location
5. **Uncover** — Each new location reveals lore, abilities, and connections to more gates

---

## 🌍 World Structure
- **Realms** — Distinct biomes inspired by sacred Japanese landscapes (cedar forests, volcanic peaks, ocean caves, floating shrines)
- **Gate Network** — A web of torii connections; not linear — players choose their path
- **The In-Between** — A brief liminal space you pass through during each jump, which changes based on your journey so far

### Torii Travel: Space & Time
Torii gates are not just spatial portals — they can transport you through **time** as well as **place**. A gate in a ruined temple might take you to the same location 500 years ago when it was thriving, or to a completely different sacred site in the present. This means:
- **Same place, different era** — See a location in its prime, witness key events, understand what caused its decline
- **Different place, same era** — Classic spatial jumps across the gate network
- **Different place, different era** — The most powerful gates connect across both dimensions
- **Time layers as puzzle mechanic** — Something you do in the past could change what's accessible in the present (a tree planted centuries ago now reveals a hidden path)
- **Visual storytelling through time** — The same environment tells different stories depending on when you arrive (a battlefield becomes a garden becomes a ruin)

---

## 🔧 Key Mechanics

### Torii as Wormholes — Core Mechanical Rule
Torii gates function as **wormholes** — they fold space-time, creating a tunnel between two points. This isn't magic hand-waving; it follows consistent rules:

- **Two endpoints** — Every torii connection requires a gate on each end. A single gate alone is dormant — it needs a paired gate to form the wormhole
- **Alignment creates the tunnel** — When two gates are correctly aligned (orientation, frequency, era), the space between their pillars becomes a passage through folded space-time
- **The passage is real** — You don't teleport instantly. There is a brief traversal through "The In-Between" — the wormhole's interior, where space and time blur
- **Stability** — A wormhole can be stable (permanent connection) or unstable (collapses after use, requiring re-alignment)
- **Directionality** — Some wormholes are bidirectional (walk back through), others are one-way (the exit gate must be independently activated to return)
- **Time displacement** — The same wormhole mechanics that bend space also bend time. A gate pair can connect two locations in the same era, or the same location across different eras, or both
- **The Kage-shū corruption** — The evil samurai have learned to collapse wormholes behind them, severing gate pairs and stranding connections

### Gate Alignment
To form a wormhole, gates must be properly aligned:
- Face them the right direction (compass/celestial cues)
- Place the right offerings (found through exploration)
- Activate them at specific times (day/night cycle, weather, seasons)
- Some require chaining — gate A only opens after gate B is active

### Spirit Sight
An unlockable ability that reveals hidden gates, ghost paths, and lore inscriptions carved into the environment. The sister's natural gift — the brother must learn it the hard way.

### Traveler's Journal
Auto-fills with sketches of discovered gates, maps of connections, and fragments of the story.

## 🎨 Style & Aesthetic — Japanese Through and Through

### Visual Style
- **Ukiyo-e inspired** — environments and characters drawn in the tradition of Japanese woodblock prints, with bold outlines, flat color planes, and atmospheric depth
- **Sumi-e (墨絵) ink wash** for The In-Between sequences — the wormhole interior rendered in flowing black ink on white, dreamlike
- **Wabi-sabi (侘寂)** — beauty in imperfection and decay. Cracked stone, peeling vermillion paint on torii, weathered wood, moss on everything
- Minimal HUD — the world itself is the interface

### Architecture & Environment
- Authentic Japanese sacred architecture: torii, shimenawa ropes, komainu guardians, stone lanterns (tōrō), pagodas
- Environments modeled after real locations: Kōya-san, Fushimi Inari, Kumano Kodō, Itsukushima, Aokigahara
- Seasons (四季) as a core visual system — cherry blossoms (spring), cicada haze (summer), burning maples (autumn), silent snow (winter)
- Time-of-day lighting inspired by Japanese concepts: komorebi (木漏れ日, light filtering through leaves), yūgure (夕暮れ, twilight)

### Sound & Music
- Traditional Japanese instruments: shakuhachi (尺八), koto (琴), shamisen (三味線), taiko (太鼓)
- Each realm has its own musical identity rooted in regional Japanese folk traditions
- Environmental sounds: temple bells, wind through bamboo, water over stone, cicadas
- Silence used deliberately — ma (間), the Japanese concept of meaningful negative space in sound

### Characters & Culture
- Character designs rooted in historical Japanese clothing: hakama, kimono, samurai yoroi armor (for the Kage-shū)
- The brother's progression reflected in his appearance — from simple shrine boy clothes to warrior garb
- Written lore appears as Japanese calligraphy brushed onto stone and wood
- Spiritual elements drawn from Shinto and Buddhist traditions: kami, ofuda talismans, sacred boundaries

### Tone
- Mono no aware (物の哀れ) — the bittersweet awareness of impermanence. The boy searches for his sister through places and times that are all fading
- Not "Japan as exotic backdrop" — Japan as the living, breathing soul of the game

## 📖 Narrative Thread

### The Two Siblings
You play as a **boy** who grew up with his **sister** at a remote mountain shrine, raised among the remnants of the ancient gate builder order. The two were inseparable — she was gifted with Spirit Sight, he was fierce and protective.

### The Abduction
A clan of **evil samurai** — the **Kage-shū** (影衆, "Shadow Gathering") — attacked the shrine and kidnapped the sister. The Kage-shū seek to control the torii network for themselves, using its power to dominate across both space and time. The sister's Spirit Sight is the key they need to unlock gates no one else can see.

### The Boy Becomes a Warrior
Alone and desperate, the boy trains himself into a warrior — not through violence for its own sake, but driven by the singular purpose of finding his sister. He learns to activate the gates on his own, following the trail the Kage-shū left across realms and eras.

### The Journey
- Each realm holds clues to where the Kage-shū took her — traces of their passage, people who witnessed them, echoes in time
- The sister leaves subtle signs behind for her brother — marks only he would recognize (childhood symbols, shared memories)
- As the boy travels through time, he sees glimpses of their childhood together, deepening the emotional bond
- The Kage-shū are always one step ahead, using the sister's abilities to jump further into the network
- The boy must grow not just as a fighter but as a gate walker — learning the spiritual connection his sister understood naturally

### The Kage-shū (影衆)
- A corrupted samurai clan who discovered that controlling the torii network grants power over history itself
- They seek to rewrite the past in their favor — erasing defeats, planting their influence across eras
- Their armor is black-lacquered, marked with inverted torii symbols
- They leave a trail of severed gate connections wherever they go — gates they've broken or corrupted after passing through

## 💡 What Makes It Special
- **Non-violent exploration** — contemplative, not competitive
- **Player-driven discovery** — no quest markers, no hand-holding
- **The gate network is the map** — you build your understanding of the world by connecting it
- **Inspired by real sacred geography** — Kōya-san, Fushimi Inari, Kumano Kodō

## 🏔️ Environmental Design Notes

### Gate Approaches
- The journey to the gate is part of the puzzle
- Players learn to read the landscape: old stone steps leading nowhere obvious, moss reclaiming the path — the feeling of crossing a threshold before seeing the torii
- The more overgrown the approach, the older and more powerful the connection
- Forgotten paths that the forest is slowly erasing serve as clues

### Visual References (from Kōya-san / Okunoin)
- Moss-covered stone torii gates half-swallowed by cedar forests
- Tiered stone grave markers and lanterns — potential sites for hidden/buried gates
- Steep narrow stone staircases disappearing into the canopy — "gate approach" paths
- Dense forest canopy creating natural corridors of light and shadow
- Stone balustrades and weathered offerings marking ancient boundaries
